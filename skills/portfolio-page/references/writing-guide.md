# Writing guide for portfolio sites

Assume a recruiter looks at your site for only a minute. In that minute they
should learn three things: what job you want, what you can do, and one piece of
proof. Read this before writing, and check the finished page against it.

## The top of the page

- **Looking for:** the job type and start date. "Looking for: tourism
  marketing and research roles from April 2027."
- **Pitch:** one or two sentences: what you do, and one result.
  - Weak: "I am a passionate and hardworking student who loves tourism."
  - Better: "I turn what visitors say into changes a business can make. In
    my seminar I surveyed 120 visitors and found the gap that mattered most."
- **Three proof stats:** a number and what it counts. Only numbers you can
  explain in an interview.

## Stories: problem, what I did, result

Each story is three short lines.

| Part | Ask yourself | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Problem | What was wrong or needed? | Guests asked the same questions every evening. |
| What I did | What did **I** do, not the team? | Wrote a one-page guide in three languages. |
| Result | What changed? How do I know? | The ryokan now gives it to every international guest. |

- Put the story most relevant to the target job first.
- "Led" only if you led. "Helped" or "was part of" is fine and honest.
- A result without a number is fine. An invented number is not.

## Avoid

- Words that sound generated or empty: passionate, hardworking, dynamic,
  results-driven, synergy, leverage, cutting-edge, innovative, delve,
  unlock, elevate, journey, "in today's fast-paced world".
- Skill lists you could not demonstrate tomorrow.
- Long paragraphs. If a line is longer than two lines on a phone, cut it.
- Private information: phone, address, birth date, ID numbers, photos of
  other people.

## Japanese version

- Use polite です・ます style, the same facts, and natural Japanese, not
  word-for-word translation.
- Ask a native speaker to read it before you publish.
- The same stories are the raw material for your 自己PR and ガクチカ, but
  write those yourself, in your own words, for each company.

## Checklist

- [ ] The job I want and my start date are at the top.
- [ ] Every number is true, and I can say how I know it.
- [ ] Each story says what **I** did.
- [ ] No words from the "avoid" list.
- [ ] No private information.
- [ ] It reads well on a phone.
- [ ] Email and links work.
- [ ] The footer says AI helped and I checked every fact.
