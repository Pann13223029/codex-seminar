# Design guide for student decks

The goal: a deck that looks considered and is easy to follow, so the
audience spends its attention on your findings. Read this before building
any slide, and check the finished deck against the checklist at the end.

## The three themes

Set `data-theme` on the `<html>` tag of the template. Do not invent new
colours; each theme already has one accent.

| Theme | Feel | Use for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `editorial` | Off-white paper, near-black text, deep blue accent | Most seminar talks, thesis progress, data findings |
| `bold` | Charcoal background, light text, amber accent | Pitches, short talks in bright rooms, events |
| `field` | Light grey-green paper, forest green accent | Fieldwork, tourism, environment and community projects |

## How to write each slide

- **The title states the takeaway, not the topic.** Not "Survey results"
  but "International visitors rate language support lowest: 2.6 of 5".
- **One idea per slide.** If a slide needs two titles, it is two slides.
- **At most about 30 words** of body text per slide. Details go in what you
  say, not on the slide.
- **Numbers are big, with a unit and a source.** Every number comes from
  the findings memo or a script; put the source line at the bottom.
- **Use the accent for one thing per slide:** the bar, number or word that
  matters. Everything else stays in ink or grey.
- **Left-align text.** Centre only the title slide if you must.
- **Charts come from your data** (the template's bar layout, or a PNG made
  by a script). Label the axis and unit. Highlight the key bar.
- **Quotes are exact** and never identify a person.
- **About one slide per minute** of talking. A 10-minute talk is about
  8-12 slides.
- **Last slide: sources, and a one-line AI-use note.**

## Do not (the usual AI-generated look)

- Purple or blue gradients, glowing shadows, glassy cards.
- Emoji as bullets or icons, and icons that add no information.
- Three identical cards with an icon, a heading and a vague sentence.
- Words like "unlock", "elevate", "delve", "seamless", "revolutionize",
  "in today's fast-paced world".
- Generic titles: "Introduction", "Overview", "Key Insights", "Conclusion".
- Walls of bullet points, or more than three bullets on a slide.
- Stock photos that only decorate, and AI images presented as real places.
- More than one accent colour, more than one font family.
- 3D charts, pie charts with many slices, charts without units or sources.
- Text smaller than 18 px, or grey text on grey.
- Made-up numbers, quotes, references or "example" data left in the deck.
- Every slide animated. The template has no animation on purpose.

## Checklist before you present

- [ ] Every title is a full sentence with the takeaway.
- [ ] Every number matches the findings memo (check three by hand).
- [ ] Every chart and number has a source line.
- [ ] One accent per slide, used for the key thing.
- [ ] No slide has more than about 30 words of body text.
- [ ] Nothing from the "Do not" list.
- [ ] The last slide has sources and the AI-use note.
- [ ] It opens by double-clicking the file, and Print → Save as PDF gives
      one slide per page.
