AGENTS.md: the brief Codex reads first

# Project brief
- Question: does "4 in 10 plants generate"
  understate how much waste is covered?

## Files
- data/incinerators.csv: one row per plant-year

## Rules
- Plan first; wait for my OK.
- Never change the original data.
- Every number must come from code you ran.
- Never invent references or statistics.

A good prompt has six parts

1 Goal
What you want, and what it is for
2 Context
Which files, who reads it, what your words mean
3 Task
The steps, in order
4 Output
Format, length, where to save it
5 Checks
How you will know it is right
6 Limits
What not to do; plan first, wait for my OK

"analyse my data" becomes six labelled lines. Examples to copy: prompt-guide/

Example 1: my graduation thesis

Japan says about 4 in 10 waste incinerators generate electricity. Is that low?

41%

of plants

80%

of waste

70%

of capacity

20 years of Ministry spreadsheets, 23,593 records linked into 1,690 plant histories. FY2024 shown.

Who did what

The AI (Codex, Claude Code)

Helped write the code that reads 20 spreadsheets

Wrote and ran the statistics I specified

Built figures, tables and this kind of deck

Drafted text from sources I had checked

Me

The question and what counts as an answer

Every definition: what "generates" means

Checking the numbers against the data

What the results mean, and the final text

Fast hands, not the brain. The thesis declares how AI was used.

Make mistakes hard to miss

Tests in the code

Plants that must link are checked every run; the result cannot change if the rows are shuffled

The build stops

If a number in the text no longer matches the results file, the thesis will not build

A mock examiner

An AI attacks the thesis, another defends it, I decide what to fix

Ask the agent to check its own work, then check the check yourself.

Example 2: a data analytics capstone

Google Merchandise Store, one year of web analytics, 903,653 sessions in BigQuery. Which channels deserve the marketing budget?

41%

of revenue came from Google's own employees, hidden as "Referral"

98.8%

of the top 1% "likely buyers" in Google's own teaching model were Google staff

89%

of Display's credit came from one corporate buyer

Ask who is buying before you model

What worked

Asking "who is actually buying?" before modelling

Rebuilding Google's lab from its own code to test it

Checking my filter against the true source: 99.2% correct

What went wrong

The AI stated facts about the lab from memory, twice wrongly

Caught only by checking the original source

Rule since then: verify before you present

"Analysis designed and directed by me; code written with AI assistance."

One rule for blanks, two different answers

41.1%
99.5%
Blank = zero
Blanks dropped

Share of FY2024 plants with a generator.

In 2024 the survey left 595 non-generators blank; in 2005 it wrote 0. Drop the blanks and you get 417 of 419.

Always ask: how were missing values treated?

Before you use any result

✓Recompute two numbers by hand
✓Check every reference exists
✓Ask how blanks were treated
✓Ask "what could be wrong?"
✓Check what each definition counts
✓Rewrite what you cannot explain

Disclose it. APU treats undisclosed AI help as possible plagiarism. Template in step 03.

Four ways to get it wrong

Private data in the folder

Interviews, personal data, passwords: keep them out

Invented references

They look real. Search for every one

Trusting a confident answer

Ask for the source and the file behind each number

Handing over the thinking

The question and the meaning are yours

Let the agent do the work. Keep the judgement, and check before you trust.
